(03)   FUE Candidate

Are you an FUEhair transplant candidate?

Hair transplantation moves follicles from a high-density, permanent donor area at the back of the head to a low-density, balding recipient area. Not everyone is a good candidate. Here are the thoughts of Datuk Dr Inder, Diplomate of the ABHRS, from Hair Transplant Centre Malaysia.

(01)   Understanding Male Pattern Baldness

The 7 stages of Norwood.

Male pattern baldness is something we treat very often. It ranges from stage 1 to stage 7 on the Norwood classification. Drag to move through each stage.

01

Stage 1 · I

There isn’t any significant hair loss or recession of the hairline.

02

Stage 2 · II

There is a slight hair loss and recession of the hairline around the temples. This is also known as an adult or mature hairline.

03

Stage 3 · III

The first signs of significant balding. The hairline becomes deeply recessed at both temples, resembling an M, U or V shape.

04

Stage 4 · IV

There is sparse hair or no hair on the vertex. The two areas of hair loss are separated by a band of hair that connects to the hair remaining on the sides.

05

Stage 5 · V

The two areas of hair loss are larger than in stage 4. They are still separated, but the band of hair between them is narrower and sparser.

06

Stage 6 · VI

The balding areas at the temples join the balding area at the vertex. The band of hair across the top of the head is gone or sparse.

07

Stage 7 · VII

The most severe stage. Only a band of hair around the sides of the head remains, and it is usually not dense and may be fine.

Stage guidance is general. Your own options depend on your donor area, age and hair characteristics, assessed at consultation.

(02)   Signs in Men

The signs and symptoms of hair loss in men.

  1. A receding interior hairline
  2. Visibility of bald scalp
  3. Hair thinning at the crown area

(03)   Causes in Men

Causes of male pattern hair loss.

Genetics

Family history significantly affects hair loss. If anyone in your family has male pattern baldness, you are likely to experience similar hair loss.

Hormonal changes

Hormonal imbalances in men, caused by factors such as thyroid dysfunction, low testosterone or excess DHT, can lead to hair loss, fatigue and mood changes.

Medications

Certain drugs, such as chemotherapy agents, antidepressants, blood thinners and beta-blockers, can cause hair loss as a side effect.

Stress

Physical or emotional stress can trigger temporary hair loss, known as telogen effluvium, by pushing hair follicles into a resting phase.

Nutritional deficiencies

A lack of iron, zinc, biotin or protein can weaken hair follicles and lead to hair loss.

Medical conditions

Autoimmune diseases such as alopecia areata, or scalp infections such as fungal infections, can cause hair loss.

Overstyling

Frequent heat or chemical treatments can damage hair follicles, leading to breakage and thinning.

Aging

Ageing naturally causes hair to thin over time, as growth slows and follicles weaken.

Environmental factors

Pollution and UV damage can weaken hair, causing dryness, brittleness and hair loss over time.

(04)   Female Hair Loss

Understanding female hair loss.

Female pattern hair loss is a progressive condition. Over time, women with hair loss may see it worsen, with a continuously widening part and thinning overall. To help women understand and categorise their hair loss, hair restoration professionals often use the Ludwig classification scale, which separates female pattern baldness into three grades based on severity.

01
Grade I
Perceptible thinning of the hair on the crown, limited at the front by a line 1–3 cm behind the frontal hairline.
02
Grade II
Pronounced thinning of the hair on the crown, within the area seen in Grade I.
03
Grade III
Full baldness within the area seen in Grades I and II.

(05)   Signs in Women

The signs and symptoms of hair loss in women.

  1. Diffuse thinning over the entire scalp
  2. Widening of the central partition of hair
  3. Positive hair-pull test
  4. Thinning of the ponytail

(06)   Causes in Women

Causes of female pattern hair loss.

Lupus

A chronic autoimmune disease in which the body’s own immune system attacks the hair follicles, causing hair loss. It is usually seen in women during childbearing years.

Stress

Physical or emotional stress can trigger temporary hair loss, known as telogen effluvium, by pushing hair follicles into a resting phase.

Anaemia

Anaemia caused by iron deficiency is a prime reason for hair loss in women.

Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy causes hair to fall out, though this is temporary and the hair usually recovers.

Unbalanced diet

Hair loss in women can be due to too much vitamin A, a lack of protein, or a deficiency of vitamin B7 (biotin).

Pregnancy

Women can have sudden hair loss during or after pregnancy. This hair loss is temporary.

(07)   Cost

How much does a hair transplant cost in Malaysia?

Cost is often the biggest question for people looking into hair transplantation. What you spend depends largely on where you live, the extent of the procedure, and the surgeon who performs it. Because it is considered cosmetic surgery, insurance usually won’t cover it.

A rough estimate in MalaysiaRM 7,500 to RM 20,000

The final figure depends on how many grafts you need.
